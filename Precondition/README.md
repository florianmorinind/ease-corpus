This paper proposes that intense positive affect, or joy, depends on a transient disengagement of evaluative monitoring rather than on reward intensity alone. 
It argues that a control mode associated with conflict monitoring, comparison, and self-evaluation can block entry into a distinct affective regime even when downstream valuation systems remain intact. 
Joy emerges when valuation is briefly allowed to unfold without immediate supervisory correction, and collapses when monitoring re-enters the loop. 
Framed as a threshold-dynamic process, the model generates testable predictions regarding hedonic tolerance, task design, and measurement effects, and introduces the Morin Non-Use Task (M-NUT) as a behavioral paradigm for transiently reducing monitoring load to permit regime transition.
