
Measurement Paradox
This document describes a core constraint of the Ease framework: attempts to measure, monitor, or evaluate a high-intensity positive state can alter the conditions required for its emergence.
When behavior becomes goal-directed, compared, or checked, evaluative load (Z) increases. This strengthens the coupling between discrepancy and correction, shifting the system away from the permissive regime.
As a result, standard measurement tools, such as real-time self-report, introspection, or performance framing, do not passively observe the phenomenon. They act as interventions on the same control architecture that governs access to it.
The paradox is therefore methodological: the more precisely a state is targeted or measured, the less likely it is to occur in its original form.
This document outlines the implications of this constraint for experimental design and interpretation.
