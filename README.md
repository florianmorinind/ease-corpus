The Ease Corpus/A Regime Theory of Joy

A theoretical and experimental framework proposing that access to high-intensity positive affect does not depend on reward maximization, but on a reduction of evaluative control.

The core claim is that many affective states, including what is commonly called “joy”, are not produced by better outcomes, but become accessible when the coupling between monitoring, evaluation, and correction is sufficiently reduced. 

The system continuously monitors discrepancies between expected and observed states. In standard conditions, these discrepancies trigger evaluation and corrective action. This creates a regime where behavior is guided by optimization, comparison, and outcome-oriented control.

This framework proposes that:

Evaluative load (Z) determines how strongly discrepancies trigger correction
When Z is high, behavior is dominated by evaluation and optimization
When Z falls below a threshold, a different regime becomes accessible

In this permissive regime:

discrepancies can persist without being corrected
sensory input can gain intensity
action unfolds with reduced internal comparison

This shift is not gradual. It is expected to be discontinuous, with abrupt transitions following repeated failures to induce it directly.

Why this matters

Most approaches to positive affect assume that it can be increased through:

reward optimization
goal achievement
meaning construction
attentional control

This framework predicts the opposite constraint:

Attempts to produce, stabilize, measure, or optimize the state tend to increase evaluative load (Z), thereby reducing the probability of access.

This creates a structural paradox:

The more precisely the state is targeted, the less likely it is to occur.

Experimental Direction

The M-ZRT is not designed to induce positive affect directly.

Instead, it tests a prediction:

That small, non-instrumental perturbations of behavior can reduce evaluative coupling without introducing new goals.

Expected signature:

repeated clean failures
possible abrupt transition
qualitative shift in action and perception
persistence beyond the initial context
Positioning

This work differs from standard reward-based and reinforcement-based models.

It does not treat positive affect as the result of:

reward prediction error
value maximization
goal attainment

Instead, it treats access as a control problem:

A change in the structure of coupling between monitoring, evaluation, and action.

Status

This corpus is developed outside formal institutional affiliation.

As a result, it may not follow standard publication pathways, despite aiming at theoretical and experimental rigor.
